## 🧠 How Mining Works

Bitflash uses the RandomX algorithm. This method rewards standard processors rather than specialized mining hardware. When you run the software, your CPU finds mathematical solutions for the network. Each successful solution protects the integrity of the blockchain. 

You remain anonymous while mining. The software creates a unique address for your digital wallet. Instead of using public ledgers for transfers, the system sends transaction data through Nostr. This keeps your activity private and prevents public tracking of your balance.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Does this software heat up my computer?**
Yes. Mining requires CPU power. Your fans might run faster as your processor works. Ensure your computer has proper ventilation and stays on a hard, flat surface.

**Can I run this on a laptop?**
You can, but keep the device plugged into a power source. Battery life drains quickly during processing tasks.

**What happens if I close the window?**
Closing the window stops the mining process. Your progress for that block closes, but your wallet and earned assets remain safe in your local folder.

**How do I update the software?**
When a new version appears, simply download the new file and replace your current `bitflash.exe` file. Your assets and history stay intact within the data folder.

**Is my data stored in the cloud?**
No. Every piece of information stays on your local drive. You control your own assets entirely.

## 🔧 Troubleshooting

If the software fails to start:

*   Check your internet connection. The app needs to connect to the Nostr network to function.
*   Make sure your antivirus software does not block the application. You may need to add an exception for the `bitflash.exe` file.
*   Ensure you downloaded the correct version for Windows.
*   Restart your computer to clear any locked background processes.

If you see errors in the command window, copy the text and save it to a plain text file. You can then review the logs to see if your system blocked a network request.
